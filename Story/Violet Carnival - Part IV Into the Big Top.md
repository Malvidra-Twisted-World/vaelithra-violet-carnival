# Part IV: Into the Big Top

> *"He cannot open the gate himself. He can only watch someone else try."*

The party reaches the carnival's final barrier — a gate that answers to grief, not force — and beyond it, the lair of the thing that built this whole rotten performance.

## The Dalang's Final Gate

Following the map revealed in Varquin's tent, the party arrives at a gate unlike anything else in the carnival: a wall of black, interwoven branches, twisted together so tightly that no seam or hinge is visible.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> The gate rises before you, black branches knotted so densely that no light passes through. It doesn't creak. It doesn't groan. It simply waits — patient, and utterly still.
>
> Standing before it, unmoving, is a man you recognize: the hollow-eyed dalang from the carnival's gate. Up close, he looks worse. Paler. Thinner. As though something has been drawing the life out of him for a very long time.

This is **Juval Garnix** — though he still hasn't given his name. He doesn't turn to greet the party. His eyes remain fixed on the branches, glassy and distant.

***Force Fails.*** The gate cannot be opened by force, by Knock, or by any similar effect. Any creature that attempts to force it takes **1d6 psychic damage** and hears a faint, sorrowful whisper — not a threat, just grief given sound.

## Juval's Aura of Despair

Being near Juval is its own quiet trial. Any character who lingers within 10 feet of him for more than a minute must succeed on a **DC 13 Wisdom saving throw** or suffer **disadvantage on Charisma checks** for as long as they remain nearby — his despair is contagious, not malicious.

***Roleplaying Juval.*** Juval barely responds to questions. If pushed, he answers in fragments: *"She's in there... I know she's in there... I just can't..."* He never explains who "she" is unless the party already knows Sylvia's name, in which case he flinches at hearing it, as though it physically hurts.

## Opening the Gate

Getting Juval — and by extension, the gate — to respond is less a lock to pick than a wound to reach. The party may attempt any combination of the following approaches, in any order, though success ultimately requires reaching Juval on both an emotional and a resonant level.

***Words of Hope.*** A character who speaks to Juval about hope, about Sylvia, or about regret may attempt a **DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion)** check. Success grants **advantage** on the party's next Performance-based attempt below; failure causes Juval to flinch away, and the speaking character takes **1d4 psychic damage** as his despair lashes outward involuntarily.

***The Pendant's Melody.*** If the party recovered the **Key Pendant** from the Tunnel of Echoing Memories, a character who plays its melody (a **DC 14 Charisma (Performance)** check, using any instrument or their own voice) causes the gate to visibly loosen. Juval's breath catches, and he whispers a single word — *"Sylvia."* Success grants **advantage** on the party's next Persuasion attempt.

***The Faded Keepsake.*** If the party recovered the **Faded Keepsake** from the Snail Race, presenting it to Juval and studying his reaction (a **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight)** or **DC 13 Wisdom (Perception)** check) reveals that painful memory works better than false comfort here. Success gives Juval **disadvantage** on his next Wisdom saving throw related to this scene — a deliberately painful approach that cuts through his numbness faster than kindness can.

***Creative Approaches.*** The DM should reward inventive solutions freely — a *minor illusion* of Sylvia's voice, a **DC 14 Wisdom (Medicine)** check while gently taking Juval's hand, or any other approach a player proposes in good faith. Use the DCs above as a baseline.

***Success.*** The gate opens once Juval shows a genuine, positive emotional reaction — cued naturally by the DM once the party has combined an emotional approach (Words of Hope or the Keepsake) with the Pendant's melody, or achieved some equally meaningful combination through creative play.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> The black branches shudder, then peel apart like fingers releasing a held breath. Beyond them: darkness, and the faint, distant sound of something vast, breathing.
>
> Juval doesn't move to follow. He stares through the opening, tears finally breaking free down his hollow face.
>
> *"I can't... I can't go in there. Please. Bring her back."*

***Juval's Role.*** Juval cannot enter Belgruth's lair himself — some binding, unspoken and unexplained, keeps him rooted at the threshold. He remains here as a witness, watching the gate, until the party returns.

## Crossing the Threshold

> [!info] Read Aloud
> The moment you step past the gate, the air changes — thick, cloying, and wrong. A wave of pressure rolls over you, pressing against your chest like a hand closing slowly around your heart.

Each character makes a **DC 13 Constitution saving throw**, taking **1d6 psychic damage** on a failure and half as much on a success, as the demiplane's true nature — Belgruth's own domain — asserts itself over the party.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> Beyond the pressure, a vast tent opens up around you — larger inside than anything you saw from outside. A single stage stands at its center, lit by a single dying spotlight.
>
> Chained to that stage, weak but breathing, is the woman from your very first vision.
>
> **Sylvia.**
>
> And from the shadows at the tent's edge, something enormous begins, unhurried, to move.

**Belgruth**, the Grand Puppeteer, steps into the light — for now, still wearing its human disguise.

> *"Ohh~ You made it all this way. How... touching."*
>
> *"Come to rescue the girl? How wonderfully predictable."*

## Ending the Chapter

**What the Party Knows.** Sylvia is alive, chained, and within reach — but Belgruth stands between them, and it hasn't shown its true face yet. The final confrontation begins now.

Continue to *Part V: The Invitation to Smile, Fulfilled* for the battle against Belgruth and the adventure's conclusion.

## Running the Final Battle

The confrontation with Belgruth unfolds in two phases and demands a different kind of victory than a simple kill: Sylvia's survival matters as much as Belgruth's defeat.

### Phase 1: Belgruth, The Grand Puppeteer

Belgruth opens the fight in its glamored human form (see *Appendix B: Creatures* for its full stat block). The goal of this phase isn't to reduce Belgruth to 0 hit points — it's to bring it to **50% of its hit points** while keeping Sylvia alive.

***Sylvia's Peril.*** Sylvia begins the fight at 0 hit points, chained to the stage, making death saving throws on each of her turns like any dying creature. Her chains have **AC 15** and **20 hit points**, and can be broken with a successful attack action against them or a **DC 15 Strength (Athletics)** or **Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)** check. Freeing her doesn't instantly heal her — it only removes her from immediate danger and stops her death saves from being a battlefield hazard the party must keep returning to.

***Running the Combat.*** Belgruth uses its **Grand Puppeteer** trait to command Gluttonettes into the fight and leans on its **Aura of Temptation** and **Puppeteer's Gaze** to wear the party down through fear and doubt rather than raw damage — this phase should feel like a battle of will as much as of blades.

### The Transition

When Belgruth is reduced to **50% of its hit points** (85 or fewer), the fight pauses for a single, visceral beat.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> Belgruth's human disguise splits down the center like tearing fabric. Beneath it: something vast, maroon-skinned, and utterly wrong, unfolding out of a shape too small to have ever contained it. Its "voice," when it finally speaks again, is no longer sweet.
>
> *"Fine then. Let's stop pretending~"*

Belgruth transforms into its true form — **Belgruth, The Ravenous Maw** — carrying its remaining hit points over into its new, larger pool.

***Running Phase 2.*** Belgruth summons **2–3 Gluttonettes** as reinforcements (two of Tier 1 and Tier 2 for a standard fight, or all three tiers together for a harder one), and may optionally call the **Juval Simulacrum** (the Masked Puppet, CR 3) as an additional disruptor if the table wants a longer fight. Its **Aura of Despair** grows stronger here, and its trailing slime creates difficult terrain across the stage. This phase should feel like Belgruth has stopped performing and started devouring.

### Playtest Failsafe: Varquin's Intervention

***When to Use This.*** If two or more player characters are unconscious and the fight looks genuinely unsalvageable, don't let a single bad round end the story. Trigger this failsafe.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> A crack splits the air. **Varquin** appears at the edge of the stage, no longer smiling. She presses the Masked Puppet's shattered mask to her chest, and violet light floods the tent.

All unconscious player characters wake at **1 hit point**. All conscious player characters heal to their **maximum hit points**. Every player character regains **half their spent spell slots** (rounded up). Varquin herself becomes **Unconscious** and **Incapacitated** as her mask cracks further and loses its power — this rescue costs her something real, and she cannot repeat it.

### Epilogue Beat

When Belgruth is finally reduced to 0 hit points:

> [!info] Read Aloud
> Belgruth doesn't fall so much as it comes apart — bursting in a wave of purple smoke and thick, rotten slime. The chains binding Sylvia crumble to dust in the same instant.
>
> She sags forward, weak, but breathing — and alive.

A rift tears open in the air above the ruined stage, and three armored, featureless figures step through: **Marut**, **Kolyarut**, and **Quarut**, the Inevitables.

> *"Contract violated. Pact broken. Law enforced."*

**Marut** seizes what remains of Belgruth without ceremony. **Quarut** turns to Varquin — wherever she is, conscious or not — and offers a slow, formal bow. Without another word, the three vanish, dragging Belgruth's remains with them. Above the tent, the carnival's violet sky begins, for the first time, to shift toward the pale color of an ordinary dawn.
