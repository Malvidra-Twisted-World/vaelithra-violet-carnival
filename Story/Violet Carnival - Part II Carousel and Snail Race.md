# Part II: Carousel and Snail Race

> *"Si Rakus berusaha mengulang pesta... temukan angka yang rusak."*

Deeper into the Violet Carnival, the party meets two of its keepers face to face — and finds the first two attractions are not separate amusements, but two halves of a single, unfolding puzzle.

## The Carnival Grounds

> [!info] Read Aloud
> The path opens into a wide fairground, far larger than it looked from the gate. Rows of game booths and smaller tents stretch out in every direction — most of them dark, canvas torn, poles snapped, games long since abandoned to dust and violet mist. Only a handful of lights still burn among the ruin.

***Running the Grounds.*** The Violet Carnival was once much bigger than what the party can actually use. Feel free to describe collapsed ring-toss booths, a dead Ferris wheel, a fortune-scale with a cracked dial — whatever dresses the scene — but only four attractions in the whole carnival are still functional: the **Carousel**, the **Giant Snail Race**, the **Tunnel of Echoing Memories**, and **Varquin's Tarot Tent**. This is intentional, not a gap to cover for. If the party heads straight for the carousel and snail race without poking through the ruined booths first, nothing is lost — the puzzle chain is meant to be followed in roughly that order regardless of how much of the dead fairground they stop to look at along the way. Don't feel obligated to steer them toward exploration they haven't asked for.

***Optional Flavor.*** For a party that does want to wander the ruins, a character who searches a collapsed booth finds nothing mechanically useful, but the DM can offer a small, purely atmospheric detail: a name half-scratched off a game sign, a child's drawing pinned inside a torn tent flap, a prize-wall of stuffed animals gone gray with mildew. These are set dressing only — memory and mood, not clues or loot — and exist so curious players have something to find without derailing the adventure's pacing.

## Approaching the Carousel

> [!info] Read Aloud
> Ahead, a great carousel turns slowly under strings of violet lantern-light. Its mounts aren't horses, but unicorns carved in aged wood — cracked paint, chipped horns, glass eyes that seem to follow you. The melody drifting from it is sweet, familiar... and just slightly out of tune.
>
> Beside the carousel stands a small ticket booth, its counter empty. A hand-lettered sign reads: *"Miss Lilith — back in a moment~"*

## Juval and Varquin at the Gate

Before the party can approach the booth, two figures step out from between the tents to greet them.

The first is a gaunt man in a **dalang**'s (puppeteer's) formal coat, his motions stiff and mechanical, like a marionette worked by unseen strings. His eyes are hollow, rimmed with exhaustion that never seems to lift. He does not introduce himself by name.

> *"Welcome... to the Violet Carnival."*
>
> *"Enjoy yourselves... while you still can."*

The second is a **female clown**, tall and elegant beneath her paint and ruffled collar — the same figure the party glimpsed watching them at the outer gate. She, too, withholds her name, though her eyes linger on the party with an attention that feels almost protective.

> *"Oh, don't mind him. He's always like this."*
>
> *"My advice? Play along. Smile. The Carnival likes smiles."*

***Running These NPCs.*** This is the party's first real meeting with **Juval** (the dalang) and **Varquin** (the clown woman), though neither name is given yet — let the players refer to them however they like. Play Juval as hollowed-out and joyless, a man going through motions he no longer believes in. Play Varquin as warm, a little too warm, watching the party with quiet purpose she never explains. Neither will answer direct questions about themselves; Juval deflects with silence, Varquin with a joke.

After a moment, Varquin gestures toward the carousel.

> *"Miss Lilith runs the carousel — she's stepped away, it seems. Feel free to try it yourselves. Everyone loves a ride."*

She and Juval withdraw into the crowd, the same way Varquin vanished at the outer gate.

## The Carousel: A Trap Springs

When the party approaches the empty booth and inserts their tickets (spending **1 bite** to activate the ride), the carousel responds.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> The carousel shudders to life on its own. Violet light floods its central pole, and for one instant, a small **masked figure** — child-sized, faceless — appears atop it, points toward one of the unicorns, and vanishes.
>
> Then the ride screams.
>
> The carousel spins faster than any machine should, gears grinding, unicorns rearing as if alive. A dome of solid violet light seals down — not just over the platform, but the whole clearing around it, sweeping up everyone standing nearby into the barrier with a jolt.

A cold, telepathic whisper touches every mind inside the dome:

> *"You have chosen your fate. Only answers lead you out."*

***No One Gets Left Outside.*** The dome's radius is generous by design — anyone still in the general area when the ride triggers (browsing a nearby booth, hanging back, keeping watch) gets pulled in along with the rest of the party. This is deliberate: the puzzle is built for the whole table to solve together, and no character should end up stuck outside watching the others work through it. The only way to avoid the dome entirely is to have deliberately walked well out of sight of the carousel *before* the ride activates — a player choosing that has, in effect, chosen not to be part of this scene, and the DM should treat it as a real choice, not an accident.

***If a Player Opts Out Anyway.*** Occasionally a player will choose to hang back regardless — wandering the ruined booths from *The Carnival Grounds*, or staying to watch the gate. If that happens, don't let them go idle: give them a small parallel beat instead. A good option is having them spot **Miss Lilith** at a distance — the missing carousel keeper, watching the trapped party from behind a collapsed tent, saying nothing, vanishing the moment she's approached directly. It doesn't solve anything mechanically, but it hands that player their own thread to chase (why is she avoiding the carousel? where does she go?) instead of leaving them with nothing to do while the table works the puzzle.

***Running the Trap.*** Any character who tries to force their way past the dome — by touching it, attacking it, or attempting to leave the platform — takes **1d6 psychic damage** and hears the whisper repeat. The carousel keeps spinning throughout; a character can attempt a **DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics)** check to move safely between mounts, or a **DC 12 Strength (Athletics)** check to hold steady while examining one. Failure on either doesn't cause harm, just wasted effort — momentum, not violence, is the danger here.

## The Four Unicorns

Four unicorn pairs stand around the carousel, each bearing a partial nameplate and a small riddle plaque. Speaking both correct names of a pair aloud (in any order) calms that pair and reveals a hidden token bearing one of the words.

***Gilded Gluttons.*** The plaque reads: *"I hoard until I am consumed by my own fire — I want everything, and so I lose everything."* The correct answer is **Keabadian** (Eternity) — etched on a broken horn shard's outer face — and **Kelimpahan** (Abundance) — hidden on the shard's inner, hidden face, found by turning the shard over.

***Shadowed Seekers.*** The plaque reads: *"I chase the line where sky meets sea, and never once arrive."* A character who cleans the grime from the small mirror mounted on the carousel's central pole sees an illusion of open ocean — the word **Cakrawala** (Horizon). Touching the mirror sends a telepathic whisper carrying the second word, **Ketiadaan** (Nothingness).

***Frozen Weepers.*** The plaque reads: *"I wear a smile that never once knew tears."* A silk handkerchief tucked into the saddle bears the embroidered word **Keriangan** (Joy). Warming the handkerchief near a heat source (a candle, a torch, the spell *control flame*, or similar) reveals a second word beneath, written in ice-crystal frost: **Kepedihan** (Sorrow).

***Sinner's Steeds.*** The plaque reads: *"I ride the same road forever, and call it a journey."* A small card attached to a music box on the saddle reads **Siklus** (Cycle). Forcing the music box open (**DC 13 Strength check**, or a successful **DC 12 Sleight of Hand** to work the catch without breaking it) reveals a second word engraved inside its gear mechanism: **Pengulangan** (Repetition).

***Success.*** When all four pairs are named correctly, the carousel shudders to a halt and the violet dome dissolves. A masked figure's voice — child-like, distant — speaks once more through the unicorns' stilled mouths:

> *"Si Rakus berusaha mengulang pesta..."*
> *(The Glutton tries to relive the feast...)*
>
> *"Temukan angka yang rusak di Balap Siput."*
> *(Find the broken number at the Snail Race.)*

***Vision of Sylvia.*** As the message fades, every character present catches a brief, shared vision: a woman bound in a dark tent, chained to a stage, weeping silently. It's the same woman from the party's very first vision — proof, for anyone who doubted it, that she's real, and close.

***Carousel Reward.*** Once the barrier falls, a small compartment opens beneath the central pole. Roll or choose from the table below (see *Appendix A: Magic Items* for full item descriptions):

| 2 | Cracked Mirror Shard |
| 3 | Frostbitten Ribbon |

## The Snail Race

Following the unicorns' message, the party finds the Giant Snail Race a short walk from the carousel.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> A wide dirt track curves ahead, lined with cheering violet lanterns shaped like tiny faces. At the starting line waits an old man on one leg, leaning on a crutch fashioned from a snail's shell. Behind him, three enormous snails wait patiently, their shells gleaming with faint runes.

This is **Old Man Thoraq**, keeper of the Giant Snail Race. He grins, missing several teeth, and gestures grandly at his snails.

> *"Care for a race, little travelers? Pick your steed — Rocket, Sluggernaut, or ol' Lendir Jahat there. Winner takes home a prize!"*

***Roleplaying Thoraq.*** Thoraq is jovial, a little too eager, and never explains why a one-legged man runs a snail race in a demonic carnival. If pressed, he just laughs it off: *"Everybody's gotta work somewhere, eh?"* He does not know what the race truly reveals — to him, it's just a game.

***Choosing a Snail.*** Each character picks one of Thoraq's three snails to ride. Their personalities matter — a character whose actions during the race match their snail's temperament gains advantage on that round's check (see *Running the Race*, below).

| Snail | Personality | What Earns Advantage |
|---|---|---|
| **Rocket** | Impatient and easily bored; strains against the reins the moment the race isn't moving fast enough. | Aggressive urging — shouting, kicking off, promising a reward for speed. |
| **Sluggernaut** | Stubborn and food-motivated; will not be rushed, but will absolutely be bribed. | Offering (or pretending to offer) food, treats, or anything edible-looking. |
| **Lendir Jahat** ("Wicked Slime") | Skittish and easily spooked; responds better to calm reassurance than commands. | Gentle, soothing encouragement — a soft voice, a steady hand, patience. |

A character can also ride bareback with no particular strategy and simply make a straightforward Animal Handling check — the personality match only ever helps, it's never required.

***Entering the Race.*** Entry costs **1 bite** per participating character. Thoraq pours a shimmering purple liquid over each racer's head, and they shrink rapidly to the size of a snail themselves, climbing onto their chosen mount's shell.

## Running the Race

The race lasts **5 rounds**. Each round, a racing character makes an **Animal Handling check (DC 15)** to urge their snail forward:

- **Success:** the snail advances 5 inches along the track.
- **Natural 20:** the snail advances 10 inches.
- **Natural 1:** the snail slips back 5 inches.
- **Failure (non-natural-1):** no movement.

A character who takes a creative, personality-matched action toward their snail (see the snail's motivation, at the DM's discretion) gains **advantage** on that round's check and an extra 5 inches of movement on success.

***Visions During the Race.*** At the end of each round, regardless of success or failure, every racing character shares a brief, silent vision tied to that round's distance:

- **Round 1 (5 in.) — the number 10.** A vision of the carnival's early glory: ledgers and coin-counts written in violet "blood," numbers climbing without end. This is Belgruth's promise of endless wealth — the seed of Juval's downfall.
- **Round 2 (10 in.) — the number 2.** A vision of Juval hiding, weeping, two newly-made **Gluttonettes** standing silent guard beside him. This is the moment he accepted the demon's help.
- **Round 3 (15 in.) — the number 3.** A vision of Sylvia being dragged toward a stage. Before she vanishes into darkness, she holds up three fingers. This is a hint that three attractions — the Carousel, this race, and what waits ahead — together hold the truth.
- **Round 4 (20 in.) — the number 0.** A vision of Belgruth in human form, holding four empty silver plates. A voice murmurs: *"Yang tersisa hanyalah kosong." (What remains is empty.)* This is the shape of total consumption — zero souls left behind.
- **Round 5 (25 in.) — the finish.** A vision of the track itself, unshrunk and empty, four numbered starting slots along its edge — and one snail missing from the fifth.

***Ending the Race.*** After round 5, the liquid's effect fades and every character returns to full size, whichever snail is "ahead" being purely cosmetic — the vision sequence matters far more than who wins.

## The Score Board

Beside the finish line stands a wooden **Score Board** with four empty slots, each shaped to hold a single carved numeral tile. A basket of loose tiles (0 through 9, several of each) sits nearby.

***Solving the Board.*** The correct sequence, drawn directly from the race's visions, is **10, 2, 3, 0**. Entering any other order does nothing — no penalty, no lockout, just silence. A character who succeeds on a **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** check while examining the visions afterward recalls the order of the numbers even without perfect memory of the visions themselves.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> The moment the final tile clicks into place, the lanterns along the track flash red, then green. A heavy click sounds beneath your feet. Slowly, the wooden track itself rotates a full half-turn, revealing a hidden door carved with the imagery of tarot cards.
>
> Across its surface, in curling script, are the words:
>
> *"The card always lies, but the pattern never does."*

***Snail Race Reward.*** Whichever character's snail crossed the line first receives the **Faded Keepsake** — a dull glass marble bearing a faint symbol from the Archive of Vaeil (see *Appendix A: Magic Items*).

## Ending the Chapter

**What the Party Knows.** The carnival's attractions aren't separate games — they're pieces of one larger message, and someone (or something) wants the party to find it. A hidden door now stands open beneath the snail track, leading toward a fortune-teller's tent and the next piece of the puzzle.
