# Violet Carnival: The Invitation to Smile

> *"Everyone here has room for one more smile."*

## Adventure Information

> ##### Adventure Information
> **Recommended Level:** 5
> **Party Size:** 4–6
> **Play Time:** 6–8 hours
> **Setting:** A demiplane beyond ordinary time — the Violet Carnival
> **Canon:** Vaelithra Saga III

## Overview

The party receives a mysterious invitation to a carnival that appears in no history, no atlas, no memory but their own. The envelope pulls them into the Violet Carnival's demiplane — a place that looks joyful on its surface and hides despair, stolen souls, and a rotting bargain underneath. Behind the laughter and the colored lights, someone is begging to be saved.

*Violet Carnival* is a five-Part investigative horror one-shot built around **attractions instead of dungeon rooms**: a carousel, a giant snail race, a memory tunnel, and a tarot tent, each hiding a piece of the truth behind a captured woman, a broken bargain, and the demon who profits from both. It plays best as pure mystery and roleplay for its first half, before turning into a desperate, emotional rescue and a two-phase boss fight in its second.

## Adventure Background

### The Sundering War and Vaeil's Intervention

The world of Dewanegara was once ravaged by the **Sundering War**, a prolonged conflict fought between the Gods and the Demons. **Vaeil**, the God of Time and Keeper of the Archives, stood apart, observing the destructive stalemate. Concerned that the war would end in mutual catastrophe, Vaeil intervened, subtly manipulating the currents of time to create a decisive outcome: the Gods emerged victorious, while the Demons were spared total annihilation.

This manipulation was known to powerful entities on both sides. The Gods, intent on preserving Vaeil's rewritten Archives to secure their victory, and the Demons, seeking to control the Archives to seize power, both coveted Vaeil's domain. To safeguard his work and the balance he created, **Vaeil shattered his own soul into twelve fragments — the Vaelithra** — and scattered them across Dewanegara, and sometimes beyond time itself.

### The Dalang and the Devil

The Violet Carnival runs every year, though no one can say when it began — only those who have visited remember it exists at all, and even their memories fade the moment they return to the material plane.

The carnival belongs to a "Dalang" (puppeteer-showman) named **Juval Garnix**, an elf Warlock who dreamed of building a place where children could laugh alongside their families. His wife **Sylvia Garnix**, a devoted half-elf Cleric, supported him at every step — but every attempt at construction was cursed with failure.

Then, one day, a man appeared before the struggling Dalang. He promised to help realize the Violet Carnival, even to make it grander than Juval had ever imagined. His smile seemed genuine, his words persuasive — and he asked for nothing in payment. No gold, no favor. Only one condition: he would be allowed to visit the carnival from time to time, and no one was ever to ask why.

It seemed a small price. Juval shook his hand.

What Juval didn't know was that the stranger was no man at all — he was **Belgruth**, a demon of the Domain of Glutton, one of the **Little Sins** subordinate to the greater **Deadly Sins** pantheon. Belgruth introduced himself simply as "Bel." His true purpose was never charity — it was cultivation. He wanted a *feeding ground*.

Rather than simply visiting, Belgruth began abducting carnival guests and devouring their souls, leaving behind hollow, mask-wearing husks — the **Gluttonettes** — that he sent back into the carnival as staff, performers, and silent witnesses to their own theft.

When business slowed and the carnival grew quiet, Belgruth turned on the Dalang who had welcomed him in. In his fury, he abducted **Sylvia** without Juval's knowledge. Juval suspected the truth the moment he connected the pattern of missing guests to Belgruth — but too afraid to fight a demon directly, he did the only thing he could: he kept inviting people in, hoping one of them could succeed where he could not.

Juval seeded the puzzles of his own carnival with clues to Sylvia's location, using a **simulacrum** — a Gluttonette given a black marionette's body and a white clown mask with a red tear-streak beneath each eye — as a guide who could move through the carnival even while Belgruth's attention was on him.

That white mask is no ordinary object. It is a fragment of **Vaeil's soul — a Vaelithra**. And a *human* Vaelithra already walks the Violet Carnival, disguised as a Harlequin-styled female clown, working as the carnival's tarot reader. Her name, as far as anyone in the carnival knows her, is **Varquin**. It was Varquin who quietly gave Juval the mask that animates his simulacrum, and Varquin who has been steering the right kind of traveler toward the right invitation ever since.

## Adventure Outline

**Part I: The Invitation** — An envelope of impossible violet appears before each character, drawing them into a vision of a caged woman before pulling them bodily into the demiplane. At the gate, Mr. Smiley Hat validates their Violet Carnival Tickets with a literal bite, introducing the adventure's core resource: five bites, one per attraction, with the carnival's rules made very clear — this is a place with a price.

**Part II: Carousel and Snail Race** — Juval and Varquin greet the party at the true gate into the carnival proper. The party tackles two attractions in sequence: Miss Lilith's corrupted carousel, an isolation-chamber puzzle of paired synonyms hidden on four unicorns, and Old Man Thoraq's Giant Snail Race, where the party shrinks down to ride giant snails and gathers a sequence of numbers from visions triggered along the track.

**Part III: Echoes and Fortunes** — The Tunnel of Echoing Memories, a dark ride guided by the fading NPC Whispering Lady Dima, asks the party to piece together four fragments of a broken love song. Varquin's Tarot Tent then becomes the adventure's turning point: using the numeric sequence from the snail race as a key, the party solves a card-ordering puzzle that reveals exactly where Sylvia is being held — the abandoned Old Stage.

**Part IV: Into the Big Top** — At the gate to the old stage, a catatonic Juval blocks the way, bound by a wall of despair only emotional connection can unmake. Beyond him lies Belgruth's lair and the adventure's climax: a two-phase boss fight against Belgruth, first as the Grand Puppeteer, then — once wounded badly enough — as the monstrous Ravenous Maw, while Sylvia hangs in the balance nearby.

**Part V: The Invitation to Smile, Fulfilled** — With Belgruth defeated and dragged off by the cosmic Inevitables for breaking his pact, Varquin reveals her true nature, gifts the party the **Aegis Vaeil: Echo of Chronos**, and sends them home — with or without their memories intact, but with a thread of fate now stretched toward whatever comes next in the Vaelithra saga.

## Alternate Opening: Personalized Introductions

Before the formal vision described in Part I, some DMs may prefer to open the session by spending a few minutes with each character individually — showing where they are and what they're doing in the moments before the violet envelope finds them, then having each player describe their own reaction. This isn't required; the vision-and-arrival sequence in Part I works perfectly well as a cold open. But if your table enjoys a slower, more personal hook, consider narrating (or asking each player to describe) a short "where were you" vignette before the envelope appears — a tavern, a market stall, a battlefield's edge, wherever fits their character — ending each vignette the same way: the envelope is simply *there*, and no one around them notices it but them.

An even simpler mechanical variant exists for tables that want less narrative and more immediate stakes: the envelope can be warded with *gate* and *sleep* effects. On opening it, each character must succeed on a DC 17 Charisma saving throw or fall asleep instantly; on a failure, the envelope glows violet and the character is teleported directly to the carnival gate, falling prone in front of it. Either way — slow vignette or instant Gate spell — every character ends up receiving the same short vision (see Part I) and waking at the gate.

## Secrets and DM Guidance

The following truths are never stated outright anywhere in the adventure. Use them to answer player questions consistently and to color every NPC interaction, but let the party piece them together rather than revealing them directly.

- **Varquin is a human Vaelithra** — a fragment of Vaeil's soul given human form, working undercover as the carnival's fortune teller. She secretly gave one of her own masks to Juval, which now animates his simulacrum. That mask represents roughly half of her total power; while it's out in the world doing its work, Varquin herself is weakened.
- **Belgruth hides in the old, abandoned stage tent** at the far end of the carnival — the one location the carnival's usual crowds never visit anymore.
- **The Violet Carnival did not begin as a demiplane.** It originally existed in the ordinary world. Only after Belgruth partnered with Juval did he relocate the entire carnival into a pocket dimension beyond easy reach — including beyond the reach of the Inevitables, the cosmic enforcers who punish broken pacts.
- **The end state:** once the party defeats Belgruth, the demiplane's stability collapses, and the Inevitables — Marut, Kolyarut, and Quarut — arrive to drag Belgruth away for judgment. The party does not have to (and should not easily be able to) permanently destroy Belgruth themselves; his punishment is out of their hands and belongs to a higher law.
- **The three central attractions are not independent puzzles — they are one chain.** The unicorn-naming puzzle at the carousel unlocks a hidden passage beneath the snail race track; the numeric sequence gathered during the snail race (10, 2, 3, 0) unlocks the meaning of a locked journal in the tarot tent; and the tarot tent's card-ordering puzzle is what finally reveals the Old Stage's location. A party that skips or rushes any one of the three will stall at the next.

## Running This Adventure

***DM Tips.***

- **Let the carnival feel wrong before it feels dangerous.** Almost nothing in Parts I–III is combat. The horror here is atmospheric and investigative — flickering not-quite-right smiles, plaques with missing letters, melodies that resolve a half-step too late. Save real threat for Part IV.
- **Mr. Smiley Hat, Miss Lilith, Old Man Thoraq, and Whispering Lady Dima are not villains.** They gatekeep attractions and enforce the carnival's rules, but none of them act with malice — some (like Dima and Thoraq) barely have full self-awareness left. Play them as unsettling rather than hostile.
- **The Bite System is a resource for tension, not a punishment.** Each Violet Carnival Ticket starts with five bite marks; entering a new attraction normally costs one. Puzzle-solving, secret paths, and NPC favors can bypass the cost entirely. If a party runs out of bites, give them a way forward through cleverness or negotiation — never a hard lock.
- **Juval's despair-gate in Part IV is an emotional challenge, not a lock-and-key puzzle.** Reward players who engage with *why* Juval is broken (guilt, love, fear) over players who just roll dice at it.
- **Belgruth's phase transition should be visually shocking.** His Grand Puppeteer form is charming and manipulative; his Ravenous Maw form should read as a complete loss of composure — panic and appetite with no performance left in it.
- **Sylvia's survival matters more than Belgruth's death.** Keep her HP and chain AC visible to the party as a countdown throughout the fight; her fate should feel like it's genuinely in their hands, not scripted to work out regardless of their choices.

## Important NPCs

**Juval Garnix, The Withered Dalang.** The carnival's owner and public face — an elf Warlock whose warmth is real but exhausted. He cannot act openly against Belgruth (the demiplane watches him too closely), so he communicates through puzzles, his simulacrum, and quiet, double-edged dialogue. He is not an obstacle; he is the adventure's first victim, and its most consistent ally.

**Sylvia Garnix, The Beacon of Fading Hope.** Juval's wife, a half-elf Cleric and the carnival's original source of joy before Belgruth took her. She appears first as a vision — bound, unconscious, and fading — and becomes a very real, very fragile presence to protect during the Part IV boss fight.

**Belgruth, "Bel."** A demon of the Domain of Glutton, subordinate to the Little Sins pantheon. Publicly, he was the charming "investor" who saved the Violet Carnival. In truth, he has spent years abducting guests and devouring their souls to create the Gluttonettes. He is entitled, hungry, and genuinely unaware that anything about his arrangement with Juval was ever anything but generous.

**Varquin, The Whispering Oracle.** The carnival's tarot reader and the party's true guide, though she never announces herself as such. See *Secrets and DM Guidance* above for her true nature. She speaks in symbols and half-truths, never outright lies, and quietly steers the party toward Sylvia at every turn.

**Mr. Smiley Hat.** The gatekeeper who validates Violet Carnival Tickets with an unsettling bite. Cheerful, rhyming, unblinking, and utterly loyal to the carnival's rules — but not dangerous unless provoked.

**Old Man Thoraq.** The one-legged keeper of the Giant Snail Race, a figure whose memory seems to have been washed clean more than once. He occasionally surfaces a fragment of real memory — "I've... seen that woman before, haven't I?" — before losing the thread again.

**Miss Lilith.** The carousel's porcelain-smiled keeper, obsessively cheerful in a way that curdles the longer she's watched. She remembers the carnival's early, hopeful days better than anyone still walking its grounds.

**Whispering Lady Dima.** The guide of the Tunnel of Echoing Memories — less a living person than a residue of memory given shape, murmuring half-finished thoughts at the boat dock. She means no harm; she may not be entirely capable of meaning anything at all anymore.

**The Gluttonettes.** Former carnival guests, hollowed out and masked, now serving as staff, performers, and silent witnesses. A few retain flickers of memory beneath the mask.

**The Inevitables — Marut, Kolyarut, and Quarut.** Emotionless cosmic enforcers of pacts and natural law. They appear only once, in Part V, to claim Belgruth for judgment. They are not meant to be fought, and the party should have no reason to try.
