# Part III: Echoes and Fortunes

> *"The card always lies, but the pattern never does."*

Beyond the hidden door, the party finds a boat ride through memory itself — and, waiting at its end, a fortune-teller who knows far more than she lets on.

## The Tunnel of Echoing Memories

> [!info] Read Aloud
> The hidden door opens onto a narrow dock, where a single wooden boat waits on still, black water. No oars. No rudder. A lantern at its prow burns with a low violet flame, and somewhere ahead, in the dark, something hums an old, half-remembered tune.

Entering the tunnel costs **1 bite** per party (not per character). Once the party boards, the boat departs on its own, gliding forward at a pace no one aboard can control.

## Nyonya Bisik

Partway into the tunnel, a figure becomes visible sitting at the water's edge — an elderly woman in tattered mourning clothes, her form faintly translucent, flickering like a candle in wind.

This is **Whispering Lady Dima**, known to those who remember her at all as **Nyonya Bisik**. She murmurs broken half-sentences to no one in particular, her eyes fixed somewhere far beyond the tunnel walls.

> *"...the flowers were yellow, weren't they..."*
>
> *"...he promised he'd come back before the lanterns went dark..."*
>
> *"...I keep forgetting his face..."*

***Roleplaying Dima.*** Dima never fully acknowledges the party. She isn't hostile, isn't afraid — she simply isn't entirely present. A character who succeeds on a **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight)** check realizes she is less a living woman than a residue of memory itself, worn thin by repetition, existing only within this tunnel.

## The Four Memory Fragments

As the boat drifts, the tunnel walls show four flickering scenes in turn — memory fragments the party must interpret. The party needs at least **three of the four** fragments solved for the ride to succeed cleanly.

***Fragment 1: The Faded Garden.*** A once-vivid garden, its colors bleeding to gray. A character may attempt to draw out the fragment's meaning through a **DC 13 Charisma (Performance)** check (singing or speaking to the memory as though comforting it), a **DC 14 Wisdom (Insight)** check (recognizing what the garden represents), or a **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception)** check (spotting a single un-faded flower hidden in the scene).

***Fragment 2: Shadow of Stars.*** A night sky, its stars slowly swallowed by an encroaching shadow. Resolve with a **DC 14 Intelligence (Arcana)** check (recognizing the shadow as unnatural, not simple nightfall), a **DC 13 Charisma (Performance)** check, or a **DC 15 Wisdom (Insight)** check.

***Fragment 3: Cracked Mirror.*** A mirror reflecting a face that isn't quite the viewer's own. Resolve with a **DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)** check, a **DC 15 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)** check (catching a shard before it falls), or a **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception)** check.

***Fragment 4: Shadow Parade.*** A procession of masked figures marching in eerie unison. Resolve with a **DC 14 Charisma (Intimidation)** check (commanding the procession to part), a **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight)** check, or a **DC 15 Intelligence (Religion or History)** check (recognizing the procession's shape as ritual, not celebration).

***Consequences.*** Each fragment that a character fails to resolve lashes out with a small burst of grief — **1d4 psychic damage** to that character. Fragments don't need to be attempted by the same character; any party member may try any fragment, and multiple attempts are allowed if time (and the DM's pacing) permits before the boat drifts past each scene.

## Ending the Tunnel

> [!info] Read Aloud
> The boat slows at last, gliding up against a small stone dock. Nyonya Bisik's murmuring fades behind you, swallowed by the dark. Ahead, stairs lead up toward a curtain of beaded violet glass.

***Success (3 or more fragments resolved).*** As the boat docks, a small object rises from the black water and settles gently in the lap of the nearest character: the **Key Pendant** (see *Appendix A: Magic Items*), a clue toward the Music Box heard elsewhere in the carnival.

***Failure (2 or fewer resolved).*** The boat still docks safely, but nothing rises from the water — only a smooth, ordinary pebble, worth nothing. The party isn't blocked from continuing, only left without the pendant's clue.

## Varquin's Fortune-Telling Tent

Beyond the beaded curtain, the party finds a tent unlike any other in the carnival.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> Purple velvet drapes hang from every corner, catching candlelight in slow, shifting folds. Lanterns shaped like crescent moons and five-pointed stars sway gently from the ceiling, and the air is thick with the scent of old incense. At the center, a round table waits, scattered with tarot cards — some face up, some down, as if a reading was interrupted mid-thought.

**Varquin** is here, seated behind the table, her painted clown's smile calm and unreadable.

> *"Ah. You've made it through the water and the whispers. Not everyone does, on their first try."*
>
> *"Sit. Let the cards tell you what I cannot say outright."*

***Roleplaying Varquin.*** Varquin already knows a great deal — she knows Sylvia is alive, that Belgruth is behind her captivity, and that the party has been working through the carnival's attractions. She never lies, and she never volunteers information plainly; everything she offers comes wrapped in symbol, riddle, or card. If pushed for a direct answer, she only smiles wider. *"That's not how this works, dear. Nothing here is free — and nothing here is simple."*

## The Lying Pattern

Scattered across the table are the full deck's Major Arcana, all face-down but three: those three cards feel unnaturally cold to the touch, and glow faintly violet when looked at directly.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> Three cards catch your eye immediately, each colder than the felt beneath them.
>
> The first shows a robed figure enthroned between two pillars, his crown inverted — **The Hierophant, Reversed.**
>
> The second shows a horned, chained figure looming over two smaller shapes bound at his feet — **The Devil.**
>
> The third shows a woman pouring water beneath a sky full of stars — **The Star.**

Tucked half-beneath the table's velvet cloth is an open **journal**, its handwriting hurried and shaking — written, unmistakably, in Varquin's own hand, from some time before now.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> *"The pattern is the secret. Authority must stand in the Middle. Hope must follow the Serpent. And the Serpent must stand where the Pact began."*

***Solving the Pattern.*** The journal's riddle maps directly onto the three cards, though it never names them outright:

- **"The Serpent"** refers to **The Devil** — the price paid, the pact itself, the beginning of everything that went wrong. It belongs in the **first** position.
- **"Authority... in the Middle"** refers to **The Hierophant, Reversed** — a false authority, Belgruth's disguise as a trusted investor, the peak of the deception. It belongs in the **middle** position.
- **"Hope"** refers to **The Star** — Varquin's own fragment of soul, the only genuine hope offered throughout the carnival. It belongs in the **last** position.

A character may reach this conclusion through roleplay and deduction alone, or by succeeding on a **DC 14 Intelligence (Investigation)** or **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight)** check while studying the journal alongside the cards.

***Placing the Cards.*** Arranging the three cards in order — **The Devil, then The Hierophant Reversed, then The Star** — causes the table itself to respond.

> [!info] Read Aloud
> The candle flames snap upright, burning violet. Varquin's voice — and beneath it, another voice, older and stranger, speaking in perfect unison with her — fills the tent:
>
> *"The pattern is correct. Ten, two, three, zero is the order of ruin — but three is the key. Three attractions. Three lies."*
>
> *"Belgruth demands Silence as its price. Find the Old Stage, where no sound reaches... there, the Dalang's wife is held."*

A hidden map unfolds itself across the table, revealing the location of a long-abandoned stage set apart from the rest of the carnival — the **Old Stage**, where Sylvia is held.

## Ending the Chapter

**What the Party Knows.** The carnival's puzzles were never separate distractions — they were three pieces of one confession, left behind by someone determined to help without breaking whatever binds her. The party now knows exactly where Sylvia is held, and that reaching her means confronting the one holding the leash: Belgruth.
